# Spec Research: Preserve collaborative research across the assisted phases

> Source: GitHub issue Automattic/radical-pipelines#136 (https://github.com/Automattic/radical-pipelines/issues/136).
> This file is self-contained; agents do not need to open the source issue.

## Goal

In the assisted spec, design-doc, and plan phases, the substantive research and exploration the orchestrator does hand-in-hand with the owner — candidate solutions, the problems with each, trade-offs weighed together — is reliably preserved in the phase's research artifact, even when it doesn't fit that artifact's documented format. Today this collaborative exploration can be lost to the chat.

## Context

- Spec phase (`reference/assisted-phases/1 - spec.md`): `spec-research.md` is Q&A-structured, and `## Research` is scoped to the orchestrator's *own* codebase reads. Owner-initiated questions and explanatory dialogue — often the most valuable findings — have no clear home.
- The design-doc and plan phases capture options, trade-offs, and decisions through a `## Topics` structure, so they fare better — but all three phases instruct recording *"in real time, not in batches,"* an aspiration with no concrete trigger, so material still slips away under conversational momentum.
- The spec phase's *"MUST NOT propose design or implementation choices"* applies across all steps, blurring the line between *advocating* a design (which should stay out of scope) and *recording* design-adjacent exploration that arose with the owner (which should be preserved). Each later assisted phase has analogous "don't do the next phase's job" rules.

## Assumptions / directions to explore

_(open — for later phases to confirm or revise)_

- Make the recording trigger reliable rather than the aspirational "in real time." Trade-off to resolve: *before each reply* (most reliable; risk of noise) vs. *before the next question* (a settled per-thread summary; less noise, but depends on noticing the moment — the failure mode that loses research today). Noise may be better controlled by *what* is recorded — a distilled, per-settled-thread entry, as the design-doc/plan `## Topics` structure already does — than by *when*.
- Make explicit that owner-initiated questions and explanatory exchanges count as recordable material, not just the orchestrator's scripted questions (sharpest in the spec phase).
- Distinguish *advocating or committing to* a design from *recording* design-adjacent exploration; the "don't propose" rules should constrain the former, not the latter.
- When an assisted phase's discussion drifts into the next phase's territory (spec → design, design → plan), the orchestrator flags it and recommends running the next assisted phase once the current one is complete — while the context is still fresh — so those decisions land in their proper artifact instead of being lost. (The plan phase's next phase, code, is agent-driven with no assisted form, so this naturally stops at plan.)
- For that handoff to preserve value, the next phase must pick up the research, but each phase normally reads only the prior phase's standalone artifact (e.g. design reads `spec.md`, not `spec-research.md`). How the research carries across is to be worked out.

## Q&A

### Q1 (to researcher): How do phase artifacts carry across phases today, in assisted and autonomous modes? Does any downstream phase read a prior phase's research artifact, or only the standalone artifact?

**A (researcher):**
- The three assisted research artifacts and their sections: `spec-research.md` (`## Q&A`, `## Research`, `## Out of Scope`, `## Consolidated Requirements`); `design-doc-research.md` (`## Research`, `## Topics`, `## Open Questions`, `## Risks`, with each Topic = Spec link / Options / Trade-offs / Decision / Rationale); `plan-notes.md` (`## Research`, `## Code Plan Topics`, `## Doc Plan Topics`, `## Open Questions`, `## Risks`, same Topic shape).
- WITHIN a phase, the writer reads its own research artifact: autonomous `design-doc-writer` reads BOTH `spec.md` AND `design-doc-research.md` (`autonomous-phases/2`, lines 26, 33).
- ACROSS phases, the next phase reads ONLY the prior phase's standalone artifact. Design-doc input is just `spec.md` (`assisted-phases/2` line 7; `autonomous-phases/2` line 7). Plan inputs are `spec.md` + `design-doc.md` (lines 7-8). The research files (`spec-research.md`, `design-doc-research.md`, `plan-notes.md`) are NEVER listed as a downstream phase input anywhere.
- The standalone artifacts explicitly tell the reader they should NOT need the research file (e.g. `spec.md` "the reader should not need `spec-research.md`", `assisted-phases/1` line 110).
- Conclusion: the carry-across gap is real — collaborative exploration recorded in a phase's research artifact has no path into the next phase, which by design reads only the standalone artifact.
- Added precision (researcher follow-up): the negative "standalone — reader should NOT need the research file" statements appear at `assisted-phases/1` line 110, `2` line 135, `3` line 124. By phase 3 the writer already reads two prior *standalone* artifacts (`spec.md` + `design-doc.md`) and zero research/notes artifacts. So a carry-forward mechanism must choose between: (a) the downstream phase reads the prior research file directly, vs. (b) the research is folded into the standalone artifact that the downstream phase already reads. This is a design-phase choice; the spec states the WHAT (the research must reach the next phase) without dictating the mechanism.

### Q2 (to researcher): In `1 - spec.md`, what's the exact current scope of `## Research` vs `## Q&A`, the verbatim "MUST NOT propose design" constraint, and does step 2 frame the dialogue as two-directional?

**A (researcher):**
- (a) **`## Research` scope** is pinned to the orchestrator's OWN codebase reads in two places: constraint line 25 ("Record any non-trivial findings under `## Research` ... with sources cited. Do not produce a separate research artifact...") and step 2 line 68 ("Record non-trivial findings under `## Research`"). `## Q&A` (per step 2 lines 53-56) is strictly the transcript of orchestrator-question/owner-answer pairs. The four sections are `## Q&A`, `## Research`, `## Out of Scope`, `## Consolidated Requirements` (lines 38-44). **Neither an owner-initiated question + its explanatory exchange, nor design-adjacent collaborative exploration, has a clean home**: `## Q&A` is framed one-directional, `## Research` is codebase-reads-only, and there is no `## Topics`-style structure here (unlike design-doc/plan, which DO have one for owner-collaborative options/trade-offs).
- (b) **The constraint**, verbatim line 21: "You MUST NOT propose design or implementation choices — those belong to later phases." It sits under `## Constraints` ("These rules apply across all steps", line 17). **Nothing currently distinguishes advocating/committing to a design from merely recording design-adjacent discussion the owner raised** — the prohibition is flat and unqualified, reinforced by line 20 ("MUST NOT answer your own questions or propose solutions on the owner's behalf"). The word "record" appears only in the codebase-reads sense.
- (c) **Step 2 frames the orchestrator as sole question-asker; the owner only answers.** Line 51 "Ask one question at a time"; lines 53-56 loop ("Formulate the question... Present it to the owner and wait for the answer... Decide what to ask next"); line 58 "Cover these areas strategically" (the seven areas are the orchestrator's checklist). The only owner agency over direction is step 7 line 118 ("The owner may also send you back to step 2 for more Q&A") — redirecting flow, not initiating a recordable question. The intent's "owner-initiated questions and explanatory dialogue" maps to a real gap.

### Q3 (to researcher): How does the design-doc/plan `## Topics` per-settled-thread mechanic work, and is there a concrete recording trigger anywhere (vs. the loose "in real time")?

**A (researcher):**
- (a)(b) **The Topics loop is a settled-thread unit in both design-doc and plan.** design-doc step 3 (lines 72-77) and plan step 3 (lines 87-92) / step 7 (lines 164-169) all follow: frame → propose 2-3 options + trade-offs → "Present to the owner ... Iterate until the owner decides" → **Append** the distilled entry (frame/options/trade-offs/decision/rationale) under `## Topics`. The append (sub-step 4) is positioned AFTER the decision (sub-step 3), so a topic reaches its final shape only once resolved; the recorded entry bundles the **Decision**. Topic shapes: design-doc lines 52-62, plan lines 63-73 (plan's link line adds Design decision X / Code task N).
- (c) **The "in real time, not in batches" constraint, verbatim:** spec line 22 "append every question and answer ... in real time, not in batches"; design-doc line 25 and plan line 34 "append every option, trade-off, and decision ... in real time, not in batches". **Difference:** spec appends the raw transcript unit (question + answer); design/plan append the distilled Topic components (option/trade-off/decision). Identical "in real time, not in batches" tail; only the unit and filename vary.
- (d) **The trigger is implicit in design/plan, absent in spec.** There are two timing instructions per phase: (1) the loose `## Constraints` "in real time" line (the aspirational one the intent flags — names no concrete moment); (2) the step-3/step-7 loop ordering (design/plan only), which carries a concrete *implicit* per-settled-thread trigger: append once "the owner decides", before moving to the next topic. The spec phase has ONLY the loose constraint (line 22) and its step-2 loop is per-question (formulate → present → append answer, lines 53-56), keyed to each individual exchange, with no distillation and no settled-thread trigger. **This asymmetry is why the intent calls the spec phase "sharpest" for lost research.** Nowhere is the trigger stated as an explicit rule (e.g. "append before asking the next question / starting the next topic").

### Q4 (to researcher): What are the current "don't do the next phase's job" rules, the scope-drift handling precedent, any "flag + recommend" precedent, and the phase ordering?

**A (researcher):**
- (a) **"Don't do the next phase's job" constraints, verbatim:** spec line 21 "You MUST NOT propose design or implementation choices — those belong to later phases" (the only one; spec lumps design AND implementation into "later phases"). design-doc line 24 "You MUST NOT write the implementation plan (ordered steps, task breakdown) — that belongs to phase 3", plus line 23 "You MUST NOT write production code. Interface sketches and small illustrative snippets are fine." (line 23 already carves out a "lightweight allowed touch" exception — a precedent for distinguishing doing-the-next-phase's-job from an allowed light touch). plan line 30 "You MUST NOT plan tests in the code plan ..." and line 32 "You MUST NOT write code or documentation content."
- (b) **Scope-drift "when X surfaces, do Y" handling — design/plan have it, spec does NOT.** design-doc line 22 and plan line 33: "If a scope question surfaces ... log it as an open question or send the owner back to revise the spec [or design doc] — do not decide it in this phase." The spec phase has NO analogous next-phase-drift escape hatch; its scope-related rules are flat prohibitions (lines 20, 21). **Structural note:** lines 22/33 handle SCOPE drift (spec didn't ask for it) and route BACKWARD (revise prior artifact / open question). The intent's drift-flag handles PHASE drift (topic belongs to the NEXT phase) and routes FORWARD (recommend running the next phase). Related but distinct — the drift-flag is a new variant of the "when X surfaces, do Y" pattern, not a duplicate.
- (c) **No existing "flag to owner + recommend a forward action" pattern.** Nearest behaviors: surfacing-back-for-confirmation (spec line 88, exclusions); routing BACKWARD (lines 22/33); end-of-run report ("any notes worth surfacing", `assisted-workflow.md` line 32); logging to `## Open Questions`/`## Risks` for a downstream AGENT via the artifact (design-doc narration line 48, plan line 59). The drift-flag is a genuinely new instruction shape. **Tension to reconcile:** `assisted-workflow.md` line 32 says continuation happens in a SEPARATE session, while the intent wants "recommend running the next phase while context is fresh." The recommendation is therefore a cross-session handoff recommendation, consistent with one-phase-per-session.
- (d) **Phase ordering / "next phase".** `assisted-workflow.md` line 5 "The phase to run is the next phase — the pipeline's active phase if one exists; otherwise the phase after the completed phase"; line 3 "You drive a single phase". Table (lines 15-22) confirms ordering 1-Spec → 2-Design doc → 3-Plan → 4-Code ("Can't be run in assisted workflow") → 5-Docs ("Can't be run in assisted workflow"). **Confirmed: no assisted form of phase 4 or 5.** Only `assisted-phases/` files 1, 2, 3 exist. So spec→design and design→plan drift-flags have a forward assisted target; plan's next phase (code) has no assisted form — hence the intent's "naturally stops at plan." Underlying term definitions (completed/active/next phase, per-phase completion predicate) live in `pipeline-versioning.md` lines 36-55.

### Q5 (to researcher): Is the relevant text duplicated between assisted and autonomous reading paths, and is there a shared file the three assisted phase files pull common constraints from?

**A (researcher):**
- **Framing:** assisted and autonomous are SEPARATE reading paths, never loaded together (`work-on-an-issue.md` lines 58-59 route a run to EITHER `autonomous-workflow.md` OR `assisted-workflow.md`). CLAUDE.md's de-dup rule is about ONE reading path, so the assisted/autonomous copies aren't "in the current reading path" together. The assisted-only scope is legitimate; parallel copies can still drift.
- (a) **The recording instruction IS duplicated on the autonomous side, in the analyst AGENT definitions (not the autonomous phase-reference files):** `agents/spec-analyst.md` line 17 "Append questions, answers, and findings to `spec-research.md` in real time, not in a batch at the end"; `agents/design-doc-analyst.md` line 19 (analogous, for `design-doc-research.md`). The autonomous analysts also independently restate the per-question/per-topic loop and a document-format skeleton (spec-analyst.md lines 29-34, 85-124; design-doc-analyst.md lines 31-36, 70-94). A fix to the three assisted files leaves these autonomous copies un-updated — a known, deliberate boundary per the intent's scoping, not an oversight to fix here.
- (b) **Autonomous mode is largely structurally protected from the "lost research" gap.** There the dialogue is agent↔agent and the analyst's job is to WRITE the running record as it goes — the record IS the medium of work (`agents/spec-analyst.md` line 6, 13; `agents/design-doc-analyst.md` line 6, 16). Assisted mode's dialogue is with a HUMAN in chat, which can swallow research under conversational momentum (the exact failure in intent lines 8, 13). Supports reading the intent's assisted-only scoping as "the problem is assisted-specific," not arbitrary.
- (c) **No shared constraints file today.** Each assisted phase file carries its own self-contained `## Constraints` block (1-spec lines 15-26, 2-design-doc lines 15-29, 3-plan lines 18-38) and its own format skeleton; the "in real time, not in batches" line is written out three times (1-spec:22, 2-design-doc:25, 3-plan:34) with no shared source. Cross-file references go only to `pipeline-versioning.md` (completion predicate) and to other artifacts / the Commit format. **Implication (design/plan decision, not spec):** a NEW cross-phase recording-trigger rule has no existing shared home — either restate per-file (matches the existing self-contained precedent, three copies) or introduce a new shared `assisted-phases/`-level file (more de-dup-compliant). The spec must phrase the requirement to allow either resolution.

- The assisted workflow runs exactly ONE phase per session: "continuing to a later phase happens in a separate session" (`reference/assisted-workflow.md` line 32). ## Out of Scope

These were confirmed as exclusions for this work (drawn from the intent's scoping and the Q&A findings):

1. **The autonomous (agent-driven) phase references and the analyst agent definitions** (`autonomous-phases/*`, `agents/spec-analyst.md`, `agents/design-doc-analyst.md`, etc.). The intent scopes this to the three assisted phase references only. Autonomous mode is a separate reading path and is structurally protected from the lost-research failure mode (Q5). Parallel recording instructions there are knowingly left untouched.
2. **The exact wording of the skill edits.** Requirements stay at the WHAT level; the precise phrasing of new/changed instructions in the three assisted files is for the design/plan/code phases, governed by the skill-authoring rules in CLAUDE.md.
3. **Choosing the carry-across mechanism.** Whether the next phase reads the prior phase's research artifact directly, or the carried research is folded into the standalone artifact the next phase already reads, is a design-phase decision. The spec states only that the research must reach the next phase.
4. **Choosing between a shared instruction file vs. per-file restatement.** Whether a new cross-phase rule (e.g. the recording trigger) lives in a new shared `assisted-phases/`-level file referenced by all three, or is restated in each of the three self-contained files, is a design/plan decision (Q5c).
5. **Phase 4 (code) and phase 5 (docs).** They have no assisted form, so the next-phase-drift flag has no forward assisted target after plan and naturally stops there (Q4d).
6. **Changing the artifact filenames, the phase ordering, the approval-file mechanism, or the completion predicate.** This work changes how collaborative research is recorded and carried, not the pipeline's phase/versioning machinery.

## Consolidated Requirements

Numbered WHAT-level requirements distilled from the Q&A. All apply to the three assisted phase references (`reference/assisted-phases/1 - spec.md`, `2 - design-doc.md`, `3 - plan.md`) unless noted otherwise.

1. **Reliable, per-settled-thread recording trigger.** Each of the three assisted phases must instruct the orchestrator to record collaborative research at a concrete, noticeable moment tied to a settled thread of exploration (e.g. once a question/topic is resolved, before moving on to the next), replacing reliance on the aspirational "in real time, not in batches" as the sole timing guidance. The recorded unit is a distilled per-settled-thread entry (the exploration and its outcome), not a raw running transcript of every reply.

2. **Owner-initiated and explanatory dialogue is recordable material (spec phase emphasis).** The spec phase must make explicit that questions the owner raises, and the explanatory exchanges that resolve them, are research worth preserving — not only the orchestrator's own scripted questions. The spec-research artifact must have a documented home for this collaborative exploration (today it has none: `## Q&A` is framed one-directional and `## Research` is scoped to the orchestrator's codebase reads). The design-doc and plan phases already provide such a home via their `## Topics` structure; the spec phase must reach parity in having somewhere for collaborative exploration to land.

3. **Distinguish advocating a design from recording design-adjacent exploration.** The "don't do the next phase's job" rules (spec's "MUST NOT propose design or implementation choices"; the design and plan analogues) must constrain *advocating for or committing to* choices that belong to a later phase, while making clear that *recording* design-adjacent (or plan-adjacent) exploration that arose collaboratively with the owner is preserved, not suppressed. The current flat prohibitions must no longer read as forbidding the recording itself.

4. **Next-phase-drift flag with a forward recommendation.** When an assisted phase's discussion drifts into the next phase's territory (spec → design, design → plan), the orchestrator must flag this to the owner and recommend running the next assisted phase once the current one completes, while the context is still fresh, so those decisions land in their proper phase's artifact rather than being lost. This applies to the spec and design-doc phases; the plan phase has no assisted next phase, so it carries no such flag.

5. **Carry the collaborative research across the phase boundary.** For the next-phase recommendation to preserve value, the collaborative research recorded in an assisted phase must be available to the next assisted phase, even though phases otherwise read only the prior phase's standalone artifact. The requirement is that the research reaches the next phase; the mechanism is left to design (see Out of Scope item 3).

6. **Consistency across the three assisted phases.** The recording-trigger and design-adjacent-recording improvements (requirements 1 and 3) must be expressed consistently across all three assisted phase references, accounting for the existing asymmetry (spec records raw Q&A pairs and lacks a `## Topics` structure; design-doc and plan record distilled Topic entries). The result must not leave the spec phase weaker than design-doc/plan on preserving collaborative research.

7. **Honor the skill-authoring rules.** All changes must comply with the skill-modification rules in CLAUDE.md: minimalist and concise; no duplication across a single reading path; generic (no tool-specific or issue-tracker-specific mentions); avoid unnecessary negative phrasing; describe the system as designed. In particular, any rule that must apply identically to all three assisted phases must be expressed without creating divergent duplicate copies within the assisted reading path (see Out of Scope item 4 for the shared-file-vs-restate decision).
