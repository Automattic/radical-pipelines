Hold the test surface to what the outcomes need. An acceptance criterion states the input, where the outcome is observed, and the outcome, never the fixtures, sequences, or variants that prove it; every producer's procedure gains a step that reads its artifact as its reviewer would and removes what breaks its level-of-detail rule — the spec producer removes implementation detail and a criterion's fixtures, sequences, and variants, the design-doc producer a component's inside and a proof's inputs, fixtures, or steps, the plan producers what belongs to the worker; another pipeline's artifacts are evidence of the facts they established, never text to reuse; and the spec and design-doc reviewers now treat a broken rule as a must-fix, as the other reviewers already do. The proof and test economy rule names its unit: proofs, tests, cases, and parameter rows that exercise the same mechanism with the same kind of input are one proof; the design doc holds every proof, new or existing, to failing when what it proves is broken. A reason for rejecting an alternative is a claim judged under the claims rules; it no longer names a proof of its own. Specs were writing criteria as test scripts, every later phase was faithful to them, two tests of one mechanism counted as different because they served different criteria, and each rejected alternative shipped a test of its own.
