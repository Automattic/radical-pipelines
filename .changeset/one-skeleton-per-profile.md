The producers and the implementer share one skeleton — Role, Seat, Turn, Rules, Protocol, Formats — under one rule: what is constant across a role's turns lives in its profile, what changes lives in its assignment. They keep every rule in fewer words: Parties folds into Threads, Observation is one rule per sentence, and the turn is one list with the mode as its first step. The reviewer stays the exception, on the evidence of a blind-graded experiment over 102 reviews: the longer and more structured its profile, the less often it judged what in a change is unnecessary, while bugs found and protocol kept did not change. Its profile is a few paragraphs — reread the assignment, ask the owner with a thread whose header it shows in full, report a blocker, end the turn — and its request lives in its assignment, now one template per turn, `reviewer-first.md` and `reviewer-later.md`, with no comments choosing between forms. Thread statuses drop the `closed:` prefix — `open | approved | withdrawn | answered | disagreement` — so a pipeline in flight updates the status line of its threads. Every profile re-reads its assignment after its context is compacted.
