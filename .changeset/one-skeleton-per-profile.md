The producers and the implementer share one skeleton — Role, Seat, Turn, Rules, Protocol, Formats — under one rule: what is constant across a role's turns lives in its profile, what changes lives in its assignment. They keep every rule in fewer words: Parties folds into Threads, Observation is one rule per sentence, and the turn is one list with the mode as its first step. The reviewer stays the exception, on the evidence of a blind-graded experiment over 158 reviews: the longer and more structured its profile, the less often it judged what in a change is unnecessary, while bugs found and protocol kept did not change. Its profile is one sentence — reread the assignment at every turn and after a compaction — and what it is asked, the blocker, and the end of turn live in its assignment, one template per kind of turn: `reviewer-first.md`, `reviewer-later.md`, and `reviewer-repair.md`. The reviewer opens no thread to the owner: when it and the author disagree on what the issue means or demands, it says so in the review thread, and the implementer escalates. A review lane's brief now replaces what a reviewer is asked by default — report anything wrong, missing, risky, or more than the issue needs, saying freely what is unnecessary — instead of narrowing it. Every profile re-reads its assignment after its context is compacted.
