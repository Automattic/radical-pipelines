The producers and the implementer share one profile structure — Role, Seat, Turn, Rules, Protocol, Formats — under one rule: what is constant across a role's turns lives in its profile, what changes lives in its assignment. The reviewer stays the exception, on the evidence of a blind-graded experiment over 158 reviews: the longer and more structured its profile, the less often it judged what in a change is unnecessary, while bugs found and protocol kept did not change. Its profile only points to its assignment, to reread at every turn and after a compaction, and what it is asked, the blocker, and the end of turn live in its assignment, one template per kind of turn: `reviewer-first.md`, `reviewer-later.md`, and `reviewer-repair.md`. The reviewer opens no thread to the owner: when it and the author disagree on what the issue means or demands, it says so in the review thread, and the implementer escalates. A review lane's brief now replaces what a reviewer is asked by default — report anything wrong, missing, risky, or more than the issue needs, saying freely what is unnecessary — instead of narrowing it. Every profile re-reads its assignment after its context is compacted.
