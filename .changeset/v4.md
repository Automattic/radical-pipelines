BREAKING: Architecture v4.

A pipeline has five phases — Intent, Spec, Design doc, Implementation, Review — and each artifact has one owner that answers for it: `spec-producer` the spec, `design-doc-producer` the design doc, `implementer` the code, its tests, its documentation, and `notes.md`, its resumption file, and `reviewer` its lane's review thread. The intent is the only authority. Every requirement carries `from-intent`, with the intent item it cites, or `derived`, with the input observed in intended use that makes it material; cases examined and left out go under **Considered and not required**. Every role runs with full execution: a claim about the present is `observed`, stating how; a claim about the solution is `assumed`, stating what will confirm it, and the implementer verifies it. Only the implementer writes solution code. One implementer builds the whole change, and the review judges the net change against the intent, by review lanes that see only the intent, its later inputs, the net change, and their own threads.

Roles talk through threads: one file per conversation between two roles about one artifact, in `threads/`, opened by a consuming role to the artifact's owner, its header naming the `opener`, the `recipient`, and what it is `about`. A review thread closes `approved`, any other thread `resolved`. A dispute about a derived requirement or a design decision is settled by observation inside its thread; only a disagreement about what the intent means or demands reaches the owner, as a thread to `owner` with status `escalated`, and the answer returns as later input. State is computed from the tree: each artifact pins its required inputs by identity at the commit its role's turn started at, an approval pins the intent and the later inputs and records the commit whose code it approved, staying fresh while the code moves only by clean merges of the base branch, and `rp check` names the frontier — `malformed`, `escalate`, `produce`, `respond`, `consolidate`, `review`, or `complete`. The frontier, threads, and later inputs' `target` name the artifacts `spec`, `design-doc`, and `implementation`. Production lanes for the spec and the design doc write candidates in parallel that their producer consolidates; review lanes each run one reviewer, and every lane must approve.

Removed: the spec and design doc reviewers; the build and document phases with their plans, tasks, workers, task reports, and reviewers; the helper profile and help requests; inspection-only execution; research records; review waves, packages, mirrors, lane packages, episodes, and the code delta; claims, the `unsatisfiable` verdict, and `Contradicts-input`; round counters and the assisted workflow; and the `rp diff` and `rp metrics` commands.

`.rp.md` authors move to conventions version 7, through version 6: `Agents`, `Guardrails`, and `Resources` name the four profiles — `build-worker` and `document-worker` become `implementer`, `build-reviewer` and `document-reviewer` become `reviewer`, and the other profiles are removed; each `spec-reviewer` or `design-doc-reviewer` lane becomes a production lane of that artifact's producer or is dropped; lanes lose `materials` and `after`. `Resources` names what every role may run.

The orchestrator drives roles with each tool's native primitives. The Claude Code plugin ships the skill and the four agent profiles. The opencode plugin ships only skill registration and re-supply after compaction, and registration of the profiles as `radical-pipelines/<profile>` subagents; its `rp_*` tools and health loops are gone. opencode setup now requires `experimental.schedules` and an `experimental.subagent_depth` of at least 2.
