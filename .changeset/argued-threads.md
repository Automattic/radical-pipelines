Have roles argue in threads instead of adopting what they are told. A finding or an answer may rest on an opinion — that a case matters, is out of scope, is disproportionate to the intent, or has a better alternative — stated with its reason, as well as on observed evidence; a claim about what the software does is still observed. A role opens a thread when it disagrees with an artifact it consumes, not only when it finds a problem in it. The owner of an artifact judges every point before answering: it changes the artifact for one that convinces it and answers that the artifact stands for one that does not; this replaces the rule that judged only findings adding a case, whose trace to the intent or to intended use is now what makes such a finding convincing. The opener judges each answer the same way, dropping a finding whose answer convinces it and holding one whose answer does not, and the reviewer's later-turn request says so. The implementer adds its own judgment to a reviewer's finding it carries to a clause's owner. An answer that a point stands gives its reason in the role's own words, since the opener, such as a reviewer, may not hold the clause it would cite. The opener's close distinguishes a finding resolved by a change from one dropped because the answer convinced it. A disagreement is now a finding its opener holds and its owner again answers that it stands, and what it disputes settles it: observation, for what the software does or whether a case needs handling; the owner, for what the intent means or demands, its scope and what is proportionate to it included; and the artifact's owner for anything else, whose second answer is final. The reviewer's later-turn request says the author's answer is final on a matter of preference.
