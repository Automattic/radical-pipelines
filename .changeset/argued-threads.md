Have roles argue in threads instead of adopting what they are told. A finding or an answer may rest on an opinion of what the artifact should hold — that a case matters, is out of scope, is disproportionate to the intent, or has a better alternative — stated with its reason, as well as on observed evidence; a claim about what the software does is still observed. A thread's recipient judges every point before answering: it changes the artifact for one that convinces it and answers that the artifact stands for one that does not; this replaces the rule that judged only findings adding a case, whose trace to the intent or to intended use is now what makes such a finding convincing. An answer that a point stands gives evidence or a reason that reads without opening what it cites, since the opener, such as a reviewer, may not hold that clause. The opener judges each answer too: a finding is resolved by a change, dropped when the reason it stands convinces the opener or a disagreement is settled against it, and held otherwise. The implementer adds its own opinion to a reviewer's finding it carries to a clause's owner.

A disagreement is a finding the opener holds without new evidence, and what it disputes settles it: observation, for what the software does or whether intended use produces a case, the side resting only on what could happen yielding; the owner, for what the intent means or demands, the escalation quoting the intent's words it turns on; and the recipient for anything else, its second answer that the point stands final. The recipient applies the route before answering a held finding again, and the opener when that answer is that the point stands. The reviewer's later-turn request says an answer may disagree with a finding, dropped when its reasons convince the reviewer; asks it to quote the issue's words a disagreement on what the issue means or demands turns on; and, after the author's second answer that a finding stands, to drop it unless the code does something wrong on an input someone would actually give it, or the issue's words demand the change.

A case the spec lists under Considered and not required is neither required nor excluded: the spec binds only at its requirements and exclusions, and how the code handles any input neither covers is the implementer's to decide.
